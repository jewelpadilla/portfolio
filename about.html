About

A little about me

When I started at NC State, I wanted an academic experience that would answer how humans and technology shape and refine each other. I then took a cognitive processes class, which changed the questions I was asking. I was drawn to human perception and attention, specifically, human decision-making. Once I began working in Dr. Colleen Patton’s CHAT Lab, I encountered the world of human factors psychology for the first time in a hands-on context. My experience in the CHAT lab quickly solidified my interest in human-automation interactions. Now, as a second-year PhD student in Human Factors and Applied Cognition at NC State, I study how users perceive and engage with automation in uncertain, high-risk scenarios.

My research

My current research examines when and why people decide to engage with automation and AI, and whether that decision to engage actually helps them. One line of my work asks about the impact of perceived difficulty on automation use, specifically, asking whether a task simply feeling more difficult leads people to use automation more. My findings have suggested that perceived difficulty and objective difficulty do not always align, and reliable automation only helps if people use it. I am also exploring the relationships between mindsets about generative AI in academic settings and students' choice to use AI on academic assignments.
